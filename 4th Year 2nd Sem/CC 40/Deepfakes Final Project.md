## Privacy
---
Deepfakes undermines control over identity and image, and can infringe the privacy rights of a person. As the person's likeness, face, or voice can be misused by other people without consent (Pawan)(Chidera). Moreover, deepfakes can also misuse the personal data that other people shared online for purposes that they never intended, making any ordinary online data as a means for manipulation. While in more serious cases, deepfakes are used for nonconsensual pornographic material or image-based abuse, where the victim's media, may it be photo or video, is manipulated for malicious content that can be traced back to them (Chidera)(Pawan). The privacy concerns regarding deepfakes is harmful as it includes blackmailing, extortion, and reputational damage, as fake content can spread quickly online like wildfire where it is difficult to extinguish (Pawan)(Chidera). Deepfakes also enables crimes, theft for example where criminals forge the images or voice of a person to make it look or sound authentic and use that against them. (Chidera)



## Consent
---
Moving on to the consent concerns for deepfakes, as you may know, deepfakes use the likeness of a person's face or voice without permission, so the victim may not know or have any control to how their image or likeness or identity is being used (Pawan). They also challenge informed consent by making people appear to do or say things they never agreed to, which is a direct violation of autonomy over one’s image (Pawan). Then there is also a major concern regarding deepfakes where a person becomes identifiable, this is especially true for pornography and sexual imagery. Claire Benn argues that if a person is identifiable, consent is required, and using that likeness in pornography wrongs them when they did not agree. Consent concerns also extend to political or public uses, where a deepfake can make it seem like someone endorsed a speech or statement they never made (Benn). Furthermore, deepfakes can be shared and copied without the victim’s permission, which makes the loss of consent hard to undo once the content is already shared online (Benn).

## Copyright
---
Copyright concerns with deepfakes usually arise when the fake reuses protected material, such as copyrighted images, videos, music, or other works, without permission. In that case, the deepfake may amount to reproduction or adaptation of protected content, which can infringe copyright or related rights. Another concern is the training process itself (Benabou). Even when the final deepfake does not closely resemble a specific source work, the model may have copied works during training, retrieval, or fine tuning, which can still create copyright problems (Benabou).

## Trademark
---
Trademark concerns arise when a deepfake uses a brand’s logo, name, or other distinctive sign in a way that suggests sponsorship, approval, or origin that does not exist. The core issue is consumer confusion, because trademark law protects the guarantee of origin and can be triggered when a use is likely to mislead viewers (Benabou). Deepfakes can also be used in fake commercials or promotional content, which may make a product look officially endorsed by the trademark owner when it is actually not (Benabou). This creates a direct infringement risk if the protected mark is reproduced without an official authorization (Benabou). A further concern is the harm to the brand itself, because a trademark owner may want to stop its sign from being linked to the content that they do not want it to be associated with. However, it is worth to know that IP rights can be used to ban publication of such deepfakes, and that infringement alone can be enough to trigger enforcement (Benabou).
